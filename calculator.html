02

How this is worked out

This is a price-return calculation. It follows the share or index price only. It leaves out dividends, taxes, brokerage, stamp duty and inflation — so a real portfolio would have done somewhat better on dividends and somewhat worse on costs and tax. Nothing here is investment advice or a prediction.

Real-history mode

  • Lumpsum buys at the closing price on the first trading day on or after your start date, and is valued at the closing price on the last trading day on or before your end date.
  • SIP buys on the first trading day of each month in the range, at that day's close, accumulating fractional units. The instalment count is shown with the result.
  • CAGR is the constant annual rate that turns the amount invested into the final value over the elapsed period. For a SIP that figure would be misleading, because each instalment is invested for a different length of time, so XIRR is used instead — the rate that makes every dated cash flow balance.
  • Prices are split-adjusted, so a share split does not distort the result.

Chosen-return mode

  • Lumpsum: Value = P × (1 + r/12)12n
  • SIP: Value = P × [((1 + i)m − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), with i = r/12 and m months — the same convention Indian SIP calculators use, so the numbers line up with what you see elsewhere.
  • A steady rate is an assumption for arithmetic, not a claim about the future.

Where the prices come from

The same source as the main analysis: NSE's official end-of-day records reconciled against a bulk market feed. The historical analysis page sets out the sources, the verification and the limits in full.
